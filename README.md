# Project description
The short-term effect of PM 2.5 on stroke: A time-stratified case-crossover study in Berlin, Germany

Objective: This study investigates the effect of short-term exposure to PM 2.5 on occurrence of ischemic stroke and transient ischemic attacks between February 2017 and December 2020 in Berlin, Germany.

Methods: Data from the B-SPATIAL stroke registry, the BLUME air pollution network, and the German meteorological monitoring network were used in a time-stratified case-crossover design. A conditional logistic regression adjusting for temperature, atmospheric pressure, relative humidity, public holidays and nationwide pandemic measures, estimated the effect of PM 2.5 during the hour of symptom onset and the previous 36 hours in six-hour intervals. 

Results: Based on 9,565 included stroke cases, a one standard deviation (SD) increase (10.28 µg/m3) in PM 2.5 increased the odds of stroke occurrence (adjusted Odds Ratio [aOR] 1.020, 95% CI [0.994, 1.047], p = .135). Time lagged analyses indicated a decreasing wave pattern with irregular peaks, the highest at 18 hours (single-lag analysis: adjusted OR 1.015, 95% CI [0.989, 1.042], p = .258) preceding symptom onset.

Conclusion: This study adds additional data on the adverse effect of PM 2.5 on cardiovascular health from a European capital city including all cases of ischemic stroke and transient ischemic attacks over a four-year period. Hourly data on symptom onset, exposure and meteorological adjustment variables allowed a more granular analysis than comparable studies, pointing to a short-term harmful effect of exposure to elevated PM 2.5 levels.
