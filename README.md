# Project description

We assessed the short-term association of particulate matter with a diameter of less than 2.5 micrometers (PM2.5) with ischemic stroke and transient ischemic attacks in Berlin, Germany, between February 2017 and December 2020, using a time-stratified case-crossover design.

# Abstract: 
## Title: 
The short-term association of particulate matter with a diameter of less than 2.5 micrometers (PM2.5 ) on stroke: A time-stratified case-crossover study in Berlin, Germany

### Background: 
This study investigated the association of short-term exposure to PM2.5 on occurrence of ischemic stroke and transient ischemic attacks between February 2017 and December 2020 in Berlin, Germany.

# Methods: 
Data from the B-SPATIAL stroke registry, the Berlin air quality monitoring network, and the German meteorological monitoring network were used in a time-stratified case-crossover design. A conditional logistic regression adjusting for temperature, atmospheric pressure, relative humidity, public holidays and nationwide pandemic measures, estimated the association between PM-2.5 during the hour of symptom onset and the previous 36 hours in one-hour intervals. 

# Results: 
Based on 9,565 stroke cases, a one standard deviation increase in PM2.5 concentration (10.28 µg/m3) increased the odds of stroke occurrence by 2% (adjusted Odds Ratio 1.020, 95% CI = 0.994, 1.047). Time lagged analyses indicated a decreasing wave pattern with irregular peaks, with the second largest association at 18 hours (adjusted Odds Ratio = 1.015, 95% CI = 0.989, 1.042) preceding symptom onset.

# Conclusion: 
This study adds additional data on the adverse effects of PM2.5 on cardiovascular health from a European capital city including all cases of ischemic stroke and transient ischemic attacks over a four-year period. Hourly data on symptom onset, exposure and meteorological adjustment variables allowed a more granular analysis than comparable studies, pointing to a short-term harmful effect of exposure to elevated PM2.5 levels.
